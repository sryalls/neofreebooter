# A provisional architecture

[Documentation entry point](../README.md) · [Resource map](resources/README.md)

The layers describe functions. One institution may serve several, and a small experiment need not build them all.

| Function | Proposed components | Connection to the rest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw commons | Knowledge, software, designs, educational materials | Gives learners and producers reusable starting points |
| Overlay commons | Curation, mentors, pathways, documentation, AI navigation | Makes those starting points usable and returns improvements to the commons |
| Autonomous production | Individuals, microbusinesses and small teams | Uses knowledge and tools to meet needs and earn income |
| Guild and community | Peer learning, trust, referrals, collaboration | Connects producers, learners and specialised support |
| Shared productive infrastructure | Workshops, tools, compute, premises and logistics | Spreads fixed costs and provides practical access |
| Mutual institutions | Cooperative ownership, housing, finance, care and energy | Addresses needs requiring continuity, scale or shared risk |
| Wealth circulation | Purchasing, contracts, investment and reinvestment | Helps income sustain useful local capability |

## An illustrative connection

A learner uses an open design with a mentor’s help. A shared workshop lets them make a prototype. Peers identify a customer and help estimate the real costs. Income pays the maker, materials, equipment access and the people providing support. A write-up improves the route for the next learner.

This is a proposed workflow, not a report of an existing Neofreebooter network. It exposes several dependencies: suitable designs, available mentors, safe equipment access, demand, fair pricing and documentation somebody has time to maintain.

## The boundary remains open

The ecology exchanges goods, money and knowledge with the wider world. Local circulation can be useful without every purchase becoming local. Quality, accessibility and member choice matter. A cooperative can trade with ordinary companies; a member can earn externally and contribute to shared infrastructure.

## Interfaces to work out

Who can use which assets? Who pays for upkeep? What happens when a project loses money? How are referrals and contracts allocated? Who owns a jointly developed design? How can a participant leave without losing access to essentials?

These interfaces will determine whether the components cooperate. They belong in [the research agenda](research/open-questions.md), rather than being assumed away.
