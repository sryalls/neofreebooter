# Raw commons and overlay commons

[Documentation entry point](../README.md) · [Relevant resources](resources/README.md#knowledge-and-designs)

**Raw commons** is our shorthand for reusable knowledge and material: courses, code, designs, reference works and datasets. Each resource has its own permissions; publicly accessible does not automatically mean freely reusable for every purpose.

**Overlay commons** is our shorthand for the support that helps someone use those resources: navigation, mentoring, peer groups, documentation, prerequisite learning and practical guidance.

## The assumed-knowledge tax

A course may assume mathematics a learner has never encountered. A design may assume access to tools and familiarity with materials. Software documentation may assume someone understands the surrounding ecosystem.

The resource can be excellent while the route into it is difficult. The community’s contribution is to absorb some of that difficulty and make the route reusable.

## A useful pathway

A pathway should specify an outcome, prerequisites, estimated time and costs, resource permissions, a sequence of tasks, places to get help and a way to assess the result.

For example, a sewing pathway might connect a FreeSewing pattern, basic construction instruction, access to a machine, a fitting session and a record of alterations. The pattern generator supplies one component; a functioning pathway requires more.

AI could help find material, explain prerequisites or draft instructions. Those are proposed uses. Human expertise and practical checks remain necessary, especially where mistakes have material consequences.

## Keeping the overlay alive

Mentoring and curation are work. A viable commons needs time, funding, attribution and maintenance. An apparent saving that depends on permanently unpaid helpers may simply move the cost onto them.

Measure time to a successful result, the amount of help required, maintenance effort and whether a newcomer can later help another newcomer. [First experiments](research/experiments.md) propose a way to test this.
