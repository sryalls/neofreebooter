## **Modern Analogues of Formal vs. Material Emancipation**

The trap of promising **formal freedom** without ensuring **material emancipation** is not confined to the past. It is visible in contemporary technology and political economy, and it shapes the risks facing Neo-Freebooters in the AI age.

### **Open Source Without Access**

- **Formally open, materially closed.** Many open-source projects are technically “free for all,” but the skills, time, and literacy required to use them create a barrier that excludes most people.
- The result: a commons in name only, dominated by a narrow technical elite. For those outside, “freedom” exists only on paper.

### **Maker Spaces and Commons Projects**

- Tool libraries, hackerspaces, and maker collectives often lower barriers for some, but cultural or financial gatekeeping can limit who participates.
- When projects are framed as “for the community” but in practice serve only the already skilled or comfortable, resentment builds. This mirrors the revolutionary failure: **liberation for the few becomes alienation for the many.**

### **The AI Divide**

AI tools intensify this dynamic. They promise to **“democratise” creativity and productivity**, but in practice:

- **Access** is skewed toward those with literacy, bandwidth, and cultural familiarity.
- **Costs** (subscription models, compute power, energy use) create dependence on corporate gatekeepers.
- **Perception**: for educated professionals, AI is a lever of empowerment; for those in precarious roles, it looks like a threat to livelihood.

## **The Emerging Political Divide**

This tension mirrors the widening gap between **liberal-leaning tech professionals** and **popular left movements**:

- **Tech Professionals**:
  - Previously aligned with middle-class liberal leftism, especially around social issues.
  - Increasingly disillusioned with traditional unions, which have failed to address their unique conditions.
  - Many drift toward corporatism, preferring to be “doted on pets of the bosses” than engage in difficult solidarity.
- **Popular Left Movements**:
  - Increasingly focused on precarity, material deprivation, and systemic inequality.
  - Perceive tech elites as insulated and complicit with corporate structures.
  - Growing hostility toward “guild-like” behavior in tech and creative industries.
- **AI as a Flashpoint**:
  - For professionals: a tool that expands their leverage, allowing individuals to “freeboot” more effectively.
  - For precarious workers: a looming threat of replacement, intensifying distrust of those who embrace AI.
  - For the broader left: AI appears less as a commons than as corporate capture of labour, tightening the screws on those already left behind.

## **The Lesson for Neo-Freebooters**

If Neo-Freebooters embrace AI and commons literacy in ways that appear **formally accessible but materially exclusionary**, they risk replicating the revolutionary failure: securing autonomy for themselves while alienating both elites and the masses.

To avoid this:

- **Visible Material Reciprocity**: AI literacy and freebooter practices must be explicitly shared outward — workshops, translated guides, low-cost access.
- **Bridging the Divide**: Neo-Freebooters cannot rely on traditional unions to carry their banner, but they must find ways to align with broader material struggles (housing, healthcare, basic security) rather than appearing aloof.
- **Narrative Honesty**: Admit that freebooter frameworks cannot save everyone, but ensure they never look like a gated enclave. The legitimacy of the class depends on being seen as **lowering barriers, not raising them.**

⚖️ **Bottom line**: The same cycle that destroyed revolutionary consolidators in France, Mexico, Russia, and Haiti threatens the Neo-Freebooters if they confuse symbolic autonomy with material emancipation. In the AI age, the fault line is sharper: a class that appears to hoard the tools of the future while others lose their livelihoods will become a lightning rod for wrath from both above and below.
