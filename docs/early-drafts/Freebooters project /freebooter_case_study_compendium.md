# Neo-Freebooter / Smallholder Case Study Compendium

This compendium collects historical, contemporary, and speculative examples analysed through the Neo-Freebooter / Smallholder lens. Each entry follows a consistent structure for comparability and extensibility. The structure includes: Context, Problem Addressed, Approach, Freebooter Principles in Action, and Lessons. New entries can be added as the framework evolves.

## Medieval Guilds

**Context**: Associations of craftspeople in medieval Europe that regulated trade, training, and standards.

**Problem Addressed**: Craftspeople needed protection, training, and a way to balance local needs with external patronage.

**Approach / Practices**: Guilds pooled resources, trained apprentices, set quality standards, and supported members with shared infrastructure.

**Freebooter Principles in Action**:

- - Material reciprocity: members contributed and benefited tangibly.
- - Dual-market navigation: local markets + commissions from nobles and merchants.
- - Commons engagement: training and skill standards as shared knowledge.

**Lessons for the Neo-Freebooter Class**: Guilds show how reciprocal, member-focused institutions improve viability without promising universal redistribution.

## Edinburgh Tool Library

**Context**: Community tool-sharing initiative providing access to tools for repair, craft, and community projects.

**Problem Addressed**: High cost of tools and skills gap for individuals wanting to build, repair, or learn.

**Approach / Practices**: Provides shared access to tools, workshops, and skill-building programmes.

**Freebooter Principles in Action**:

- - Material reciprocity: members contribute fees, labour, or time and gain tool access.
- - Commons engagement: lowers barriers to skill acquisition and practical independence.
- - VSM node model: individuals form voluntary connections without mandated redistribution.

**Lessons for the Neo-Freebooter Class**: The Tool Library demonstrates how commons institutions raise the baseline for participants by reducing costs and spreading risk.

## Stocksy United (Platform Cooperative)

**Context**: A stock photography platform cooperative owned and run by contributors.

**Problem Addressed**: Exploitative royalties and lack of control on mainstream stock platforms like iStock.

**Approach / Practices**: Stocksy offers high royalties, democratic governance, and profit-sharing.

**Freebooter Principles in Action**:

- - Material reciprocity: surplus returned to members.
- - Hybrid market navigation: competes with corporate giants while sustaining commons governance.
- - Community liberalism: contributors choose engagement level, but all benefit.

**Lessons for the Neo-Freebooter Class**: Stocksy illustrates how cooperatives can compete with corporate platforms when they align fairness with quality.

## Jimmy DiResta

**Context**: Maker and educator sharing fabrication projects and tutorials online.

**Problem Addressed**: Lack of accessible education in advanced fabrication and craft skills.

**Approach / Practices**: Creates open video tutorials, runs subscription platform, sells designs and products.

**Freebooter Principles in Action**:

- - Deep craft (vertical bar): mastery of fabrication.
- - Horizontal breadth: content production, teaching, digital storytelling.
- - Commons engagement: tutorials and shared knowledge.
- - Hybrid markets: product sales + education subscriptions.

**Lessons for the Neo-Freebooter Class**: DiResta shows how freebooters can blend deep craft with communal teaching and storytelling to build viable autonomy.
