# Cross-Mapping: YouTube Archetypes and Local Commons Freebooters

The YouTube archetypes of freebooters (Scavenger, Neo-Artisan, Rent-Seeker) find echoes in local Edinburgh initiatives. By cross-mapping these, we can see how fragile, entertainment-driven independence aligns with, and can be stabilised by, commons-based community practices.

| Archetype | YouTube Examples | Local Commons Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scavenger Freebooters | Mike the Scavenger, Jeebus, Thub | Rhyze Mushrooms – transforms waste (coffee grounds) into high-value produce; cooperative governance adds resilience |
| Neo-Artisan Freebooters | Brothers Make, Zorpazorp, Bernadette Banner, Christine McConnell, Laura Kampf, April Wilkerson, Baumgartner Restoration | The Leith Collective – provides shared infrastructure for artisans, upcyclers, and makers, stabilising their independence |
| Smallholder / Producer Freebooters | DIY homesteading / maker hybrids (smaller presence on YouTube) | Laurieston Farm – worker-led regenerative urban farming cooperative, embedding smallholder independence in commons governance |
| Rent-Seeking Freebooters | Vending/arcade machine operators, laundromat channels | No direct analogue yet in Edinburgh commons, but potential in co-op laundromats, fab vending, or community-owned infrastructures |


## Conclusion

This mapping shows that Edinburgh initiatives mirror the same archetypes as YouTube freebooters, but with a crucial difference: local examples are embedded in cooperative and ecological structures. Where YouTube freebooters rely on attention economies, local commons actors rely on shared ownership and use-value. The framework thus bridges entertainment-driven independence and community-driven resilience, highlighting a pathway from fragile proto-class to durable commons economy.
